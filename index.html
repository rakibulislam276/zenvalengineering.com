Global Valve Standards Compliance

Zenval Engineering manufactures valves in accordance with major international standards ensuring safety, reliability, and performance.

API

American Petroleum Institute (API) is a leading global standard for the oil and natural gas sectors, such as the petrochemical, chemical, and refinery. This institute sets up standard specifications for industrial valves manufacturers and publishes installation, calibration, testing, and servicing standards.

ISO

Of course, after API comes the ISO specifications sine both have some joint standards for industrial valves. One significant standard to note is the valves testing or ISO 5208:2008, which details pressure testing for metallic valves, and ISO 5211:2001 specifying the part-turn actuator attachments.

MSS

Manufacturers Standardization Society (MSS) is the oldest American standards organization. This standard specializes in industrial valves and fittings specifications. The materials and associated guidelines are used in almost every industry and are found in facilities for waste management, oil refining, and nuclear energy.

ASME

The American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) standard relates to pressure technology, nuclear plants, construction, engineering design, standardization, and performance testing. In relevance to valves, the valve industry is interested mainly in pressure technology and assurance compatibility between different components or parts.

All About Industrial Valve

What are industrial valves?

Industrial valves are plausibly the workhorse of piping solutions. These mechanical devices open, close, or partly block the passageway to regulate gas or liquid flow within a system.

Various types of industrial valves are sorted by power, structure, condition, and function, and your tool selection must be based on the required application. Although all valves’ purpose and function are similar, a thin line still separates them, making them distinct from one another.

Here’s a list of information to further explain the different classifications of industrial valves based on the following types below.

Industrial Ball Valve

An Industrial ball valve uses a metal ball with a hole bored in the middle, found between two seats to regulate flow. Applied in many hydrocarbon processes, a ball valve is capable of throttling vapors and gases, being useful particularly for low-flow situations.

Butterfly Valve

A butterfly valve regulates the flow of materials through its quarter-turn disc rotation. This disc is either parallel or perpendicular to the flow media, causing a pressure change. This valve is commonly used in nuclear, chemical, oil and gas, offshore, and mining sectors.

Check Valve

A check valve is significantly different from other valves because it is designed to prevent backflow. This industrial valve is often applied in refinery, oil and gas, chemical and petrochemical, marine, water processing, power station, and pipeline industries.

Gate Valve

Gate valve is mainly used for liquid flow shutoff applications rather than flow regulation. This valve is utilized in chemical and petrochemical, oil and gas, power generation, pulp and paper, shipbuilding, and mining sectors.

Globe Valve

This linear motion valve is called a globe valve due to its exterior shape. It is widely used in plant piping and fits well for manual and automatic operations. Contrary to a gate valve, a globe valve can be used for both flow regulation and complete shutoff flow.

Plug Valve

A plug valve is generally used in low-pressure, low-temperature applications. This is a high capacity valve made of stainless steel, alloy steel, carbon steel, and duplex stainless steel, making it suitable for directional flow regulation.

Pneumatic Valve

Also called directional control valve, a pneumatic valve’s primary function is to switch airflow. These valves can maintain pressure and are ideal for low-temperature operations like petroleum gas, liquefied natural gas, nitrogen, oxygen, hydrogen, and more.

Electric / Motorized Valve

An electric motorized valve uses an electric motor to open or close its mechanism. This is ideal for remote fluid control applications including irrigation, fire protection, aircraft systems, and industrial automation.
